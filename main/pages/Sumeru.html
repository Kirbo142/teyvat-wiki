    Lore of sumeru

    1. The Three Gods of the Desert and Forest Long ago, Sumeru was connected to three important gods: - Greater Lord Rukkhadevata: The original Dendro Archon who protected the rainforest and created the Akasha system. - King Deshret: The ruler of the desert, who sought forbidden knowledge and dreamed of creating a paradise for humanity. - Goddess of Flowers: A mysterious goddess who was close to Deshret and connected to the ancient history of the desert. After the Goddess of Flowers died, Deshret pursued forbidden knowledge, causing a terrible disaster that corrupted the land. Rukkhadevata helped contain the catastrophe, but the desert and rainforest were left with a tragic history.

    2. The Cataclysm and Rukkhadevata's Sacrifice Five hundred years ago, during the Cataclysm, forbidden knowledge threatened to corrupt Sumeru and Irminsul, the world's enormous tree of memories. Rukkhadevata sacrificed much of her power to cleanse the corruption. She later created a smaller version of herself: Nahida. However, the forbidden knowledge continued to threaten the world, and Rukkhadevata eventually had to erase herself from Irminsul. Because Irminsul stores the world's memories, everyone forgot that she had ever existed. Only the Traveler remembers the truth about Rukkhadevata.

    3. Nahida and the Akademiya After Rukkhadevata disappeared, Nahida became the new Dendro Archon. However, the Akademiya's sages considered her weak and imprisoned her inside the Sanctuary of Surasthana. The sages used the Akasha system to control people's knowledge and dreams. They planned to create a new god using Scaramouche, the Fatui Harbinger known as the Balladeer. The Traveler, Alhaitham, Dehya, Cyno, and other allies worked together to stop the sages. Nahida eventually defeated the plan, freed herself, and helped Scaramouche lose his chance to become a god.

    4. Scaramouche and Irminsul After his defeat, Scaramouche attempted to erase himself from Irminsul to change the past. The world forgot his original identity and actions, but the actual events of history remained unchanged. He eventually regained his memories and became Wanderer. This storyline reinforces an important rule of Teyvat: changing the world's memories does not necessarily change what actually happened.

    5. The Desert and King Deshret's Legacy Sumeru's desert contains ancient ruins and secrets about King Deshret's civilization. His people pursued advanced knowledge and technology, but forbidden knowledge ultimately brought destruction. The desert tribes and rainforest scholars have often been divided by prejudice and political power. Sumeru's story explores how knowledge can become dangerous when people pursue it without understanding its consequences.
